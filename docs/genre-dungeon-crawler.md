# Dungeon Crawler

In a Dungeon Crawler session the AI is the whole Dungeon Master. You make a character, walk into a world the AI generates, and only ever play your own part. You can play alone or with friends.

:::warning Still being built
Dungeon Crawler is in the genre picker and playable: the Planner's interview, your character, the generated world, the story turn with its dice, rest, death and the goal. Anything marked *(not built yet)* doesn't exist so far.
:::

:::tip What it's for
- A light, self-running tabletop game: dungeon crawls or open-world adventures, with no setup on your side.
- Playing a character with friends, each of you at your own seat, while the AI runs the world.
:::

## The basics

**You play your character, and only your character.** The **Narrator** tells what happens. It never decides what your character does, says or feels: it describes the world and stops where you would choose.

**The world is generated, not written.** You don't pick a lorebook: the session keeps its own world as it goes — its places and the ways between them, the people you meet, and a cairn for anyone who falls. That world lives with the session and nowhere else. Nothing is added to your library: no characters are created and no cards are saved. If you want to keep the world for later, use **Create lorebook from session**.

**The rules are a real game.** Dungeon Crawler is made for the **Vis Itineris** rules and picks them unless you choose others: checks are rolled for you, your weapons, armour, gear and spells are real items with real effects, and a table can turn the dice off (**How strict**) to play by the story alone. Whatever rules you choose, the crawl plays by them: their checks, their classes, their rests and what happens at zero health are theirs, and what they don't have is told in words (see [Other rules](#other-rules)).

A Dungeon Crawler session needs nothing to start. You may add:

- **a persona**, if you'd like to play one of your own as your character;
- **characters**, who join the party as **companions** for this session.

There is no lorebook picker: the session's world is its own.

### Your first session

1. Start a session, pick **Dungeon Crawler**, optionally add a persona and companions, and press **Start**.
2. Read the **Planner's** greeting on the **Planner** tab, where the session opens. You're in the tavern, before the adventure begins.
3. Answer the Planner's questions by pressing the choices under each one, or press **Just play** and let it choose everything.

## What's on the screen

The middle has three tabs: **Planner** (your channel with the Planner, where the session opens), **Story**, and **Planning notes**. Once you move to **Story**, the session remembers it. While you're on another tab, a dot on **Planner** means the Planner has said something there since you last looked (a secret for you, *Make a new character*, a question such as *the goal is reached: what now?*), for every player, on their own channel. Down the right:

- **Character sheet**: every player's character, as a row of faces over one sheet. Yours is first and open; press another face to read theirs (a dot on a face means that sheet changed since you last looked). Playing alone there is one sheet and no faces row. Until your character is made it says _Your sheet appears once your character is made._, and the faces row lets you read the others'.
- **Goal & leads**: the party's goal, and the open leads as a list, one a line.
- **Journal**: the story so far, kept by the Planner after each round and after each rest. See [the journal](#goal--leads-and-the-journal). Beside it, the **Dice** tab is the whole table as a log: every roll, every rest and every *Not changed* line, newest last, one result to a line, its times spelled as the Story spells them (_Day 1, 17:06_). Nobody writes there.

**Settings** at the top of the session opens its settings (Honour mode, How strict, the Rules options, On revision) on the Settings tab.

On a phone the middle's tabs fill the screen, the Planner first, and each panel on the right is one of the side views in the **Session panels** menu. See [Session layout](./session-layout.md).

**Scene Portraits**, if you add it, shows every player's character (with their sheet's picture) and the people the story has met (by name: a person met has no picture), as well as any companions. A person the world holds counts as met once the story has named them, not before. Its stat bars sit under the players' characters only: companions keep no sheet.

## Setting up in the tavern

Before play, the Planner interviews you, one question at a time. Each question comes with its answers as buttons, or a box to type in:

- **Dungeon or open world.**
- **How hard**: Gentle, Fair or Brutal. Fair is the default.
- **The goal**: shaped together, or kept secret from you.

Whether the rules decide isn't asked here: it's the session's **How strict**, chosen when the session starts.

Then it builds **your character**:

1. **A name.** Once your character has one, your messages show under that name. Playing a persona, the persona's name is your character's: the Planner doesn't ask.
2. **A class.** The Planner offers a few of the rules' classes that fit the world you chose (Vis Itineris has six; see [Classes](./rulesets.md#classes)). Rules with no classes of their own let the Planner name one that fits.
3. **A backstory**: from your persona (when you play one), told to the Planner in a line or two, or made up by the Planner.

The Planner then writes your backstory, and the rules hand you the rest, from the class as the rules describe it (here, Vis Itineris): your class's ability scores, your full health (your class's hit die plus your Constitution: 8 for a wizard, 12 for a fighter; you start at full) and your class's starting kit (a ranger gets a longbow, a spear, studded leather and travel gear; a wizard a staff, a dagger and six spells; and so on). The Planner tells you who you are: what they carry, what they know and what they can do. At a table of more than one, it also asks what your character does on a round you skip. After that it says what comes next: building the world, or, if the adventure has already started, that you'll join the others where they are now. The first character finished once the session's owner has answered the world's questions (the owner's own, or a friend's) builds the world straight away: where you start, a few places nearby, the people you might meet, the goal and a couple of leads. The Narrator opens the story on the **Story** channel, somewhere vivid.

**Just play**: one press, and the Planner picks everything for you, the world's settings included. If it leaves your class out, the table picks one, and its kit comes with it. It's offered with the first question only: once you've answered it, you're making your character together. A player who joins someone else's session is first asked whether to make their character together or to Just play.

Until your character is made, **Story** is closed for you: its composer is greyed and reads *Waiting: Make your character on the Planner tab first.* It opens by itself once your character is made and the world is built. A character finished before the world exists waits with *Waiting: Your character is ready: the story starts once the world is built.* The other players' composers aren't affected.

Each answer you press is said back on your Planner channel as a small line (_A dungeon._, _Name: Ann._), so you can see what you answered, and the Planner reads those answers as yours when you talk to it later. A friend who joins is told where the world stands: chosen by the session's owner, being set up, or ready and waiting. What your answers change is filed on your own Planner channel, never under someone else's line.

In a session with friends, the session's owner answers the world questions and each player answers the questions about their own character. Each person's interview happens in their own Planner channel. A player who joins after the world exists makes their character and joins the story at the next round.

:::note
Your answers to the world's questions and your sheet are saved as the session's state, so you can see them in the character sheet panel (and change them there, when the owner allows it: see [Who can change a sheet by hand](#the-character-sheet)). An answer that matches what's already there (Fair, when Fair is the default) changes nothing and isn't listed as a change. There is no summary to confirm yet: the world is built as soon as the first character is finished once the owner has answered the world's questions.
:::

## How a turn runs

You write what your character does and send it. Then, out of sight, the Planner reads your sheets as the rules see them and decides what your actions call for: a check for anything risky, a wound, an item used up or found, a door that turns out to be locked. The rules roll the checks and apply the changes; the Planner never does the sums. The Narrator tells the result, and your sheet, the world and the journal update.

While the turn runs, the Story shows that a reply is on its way from the moment the turn starts, and says what is happening: *The Planner is thinking*, then *Settling the round*, then *The Planner keeps the books*, then the Narrator's reply as it streams in. Stop works the whole time. In your Planner lane the Planner's reply shows *The Planner is thinking* until it starts writing, and when the world is first made the progress card says *Building the world*.

A turn costs two model calls: one for the Planner (its answer is never shown) and one for the Narrator's reply. If the Planner's answer can't be read, or comes back empty, it's asked once more; if that fails too, the Narrator carries on alone that turn.

Anything the Planner asked for that the rules couldn't do shows under the reply as a **Not changed** line, in plain words (_Not changed: Pip doesn't carry a longsword._), and the Narrator is told it didn't happen, so it isn't told as done. A change the rules refuse but could still make waits for the session owner's review, and its line says so. A blow bigger than the health a character has left takes them to 0, and to death's door.

### Rounds with friends

When several people play, a turn is a **round**:

1. Everyone says what their character does on the Story. Your line is your declaration.
2. Once everyone has, one story turn tells what happens to all of you. Playing alone, every line you send is a whole round.

While the round waits for someone, the line under the composer says who (_Waiting for Maren Holt_). There's one **Continue** at a time, on that line. You don't have to wait for someone who has stepped away. Press **Continue** to resolve the round early. The owner can always press it, and so can any player who has already declared. Characters whose players didn't declare follow their **standing order**: *hold position* (the default), *follow the party*, *fight* or *flee*. A player who hasn't declared yet and presses Continue is told there's nothing to take a turn on: send your line first.

Standing orders only exist at a table of more than one: playing alone, nobody asks for one and your sheet doesn't show it. The Planner asks for yours when it shows you your finished character sheet. If you were playing alone and someone joins, the Planner asks you in your Planner channel then. You can change it there any time.

If you delete or hide your line before the round resolves, you're no longer counted as having declared.

A player whose character isn't made yet, such as someone still talking with the Planner in the tavern (even with a name or a persona already), doesn't hold the round up and isn't in it. If everyone at the table is out of the fight (taken out, or dead with no new character yet) and one of you writes on the Story, the round is played anyway, and the story says why you can't act.

## Your Planner channel

The **Planner** tab is your private, out-of-character talk with the Planner. Use it any time to:

- ask questions about the rules or the world;
- ask for something in the story;
- adjust your character, and set your standing order.

After each message, the Planner notes what you asked for:

- **your standing order**, at a table of more than one (a guest at the table counts, even before their character is made);
- **an ask**: something you'd like the story to bring in, such as *introduce an alley*. It goes into the planning notes under **Asks**, with your character's name, and the story works it in when the scene allows;
- **a change to your own character**, such as *I bought a lantern*, which the rules apply.

The session's owner may also change any character, a place or the world, and add a way out of a place, such as *a door from the gate to the alley*. A player can change only their own character: anything else they ask for is an ask. What lands, and what's turned down, shows as a small table line under the Planner's reply.

The Planner reads your channel as a conversation: everything said there, oldest first, with your newest message last, your pressed answers among your lines. It also knows your sheet (health, conditions, what you carry, your spell slots), how strict the table plays and how hard the world is, the journal and the story's latest lines, so it can answer *How much health do I have left?* It never reads another player's Planner channel. A message you send before you have a character is yours, never under someone else's name.

In a session with friends, each player has their own Planner channel. When something is for your eyes only, such as what your character alone noticed, the Planner tells you there, starting with *For you alone*. It tells you each thing once: swiping the Narrator's reply doesn't repeat it, and regenerating the turn replaces it.

:::note Who can read it
Your Planner channel is sent to you and to the session's owner, and to nobody else.
:::

## Dice

Checks belong to the session's rules, and under Vis Itineris, the default, they are dice. Rules that decide another way (a pool of dice, cards, a spend, a contest) decide the same checks their way, and the line says how; see [How a risky moment is decided](./rulesets.md#how-a-risky-moment-is-decided). When the Planner decides an action is risky, it asks for a check by the rules' own name for it (an attack with your longbow, a climb, picking a lock) and the rules decide it: under Vis Itineris, a d20 plus whatever your sheet and your items add, against a difficulty, read its way (four degrees: a big miss, a miss, a hit, a great hit). A failed check changes nothing on its own: an item's effect only lands when the check goes your way, and the Narrator tells what the failure costs.

The results show on the Story right under the Narrator's reply they decided, one line each, a check's line ending with how its number was reached:

> ⚄ Mira — use (skill.athletics): Success — d20 14 + 3 (Dexterity modifier) = 17 vs 14

A change that needs no roll shows too (_⚄ Mira: Health −3_). A roll at death's door reads _☠ Mira at death's door: d20 6 vs DC 10 — dead_. The lines are folded under the reply, and the fold opens on what matters most: death's door first, then the rolls, then everything else. A spell slot spent reads _⚄ Mira: Level 1 slots 2 → 1_, and the change line under the reply says the same (_Spell slots Level 1: 2 → 1 left_). A way out the Planner lists again exactly as it already stands changes nothing and gets no line; one that changed (a door now open) does.

**Give to…** in a piece's details on the character sheet hands it to another player's character, or leaves it at a place. From a stack you first pick how many. The table says so on the Dice log and under the story's newest line: _Wren gives Potion of healing ×2 to Rook._ What you give joins what they already hold, so two potions and one more is a stack of three, never a second row.

- **The session's owner** can change every sheet, so their gifts land straight away.
- **Anyone else** giving to another player's character makes an **offer**: nothing moves until that player says yes. The offer shows on both sheets, and in each player's **Review** above the composer. The receiver gets **Accept** and **Decline** (_Rook Valen offers Potion of healing to Maren Holt · offered_), the giver gets **Withdraw** and never Accept (_Offered to Maren Holt — waiting for their answer_), and the table hears each answer (_Rook accepts Potion of healing from Wren._). If the giver no longer has the piece when it's accepted, the offer closes and nothing moves.
- **Companions keep no sheet**, so they're never offered as a target. Hand them things in the story instead.

**How strict.** A session here plays at **The rules decide** unless you choose otherwise. At **Words only** the rules decide nothing: nothing is rolled or counted: the Planner proposes what changes in words, the rules write it down as it was said, and the Narrator keeps numbers out of the story. Death's door is still rolled: nobody dies without a roll.

A roll line belongs to the table, not to anyone in the story: it shows as a small line with no picture or name, and the Narrator and the Planner never read it back. The rolls live on the session's **Dice** channel, each tied to the reply it belongs to; the Story draws them inline under that reply (the Messages panel's *Show another channel inline* setting, set to `table`). Clear that setting and the Story stops drawing them; a Messages panel set to the `table` channel shows them on their own, as a log. The Narrator learns what the dice decided from a separate note placed after the story so far, never from the roll lines. Your Planner channel has table lines too: the Planner saying your character's name back, noting a standing order, or *Give me a moment to build the world*. Nobody wrote them, so there is nothing to regenerate. They already know every result, and reading the lines taught the Narrator to invent rolls of its own.

A way out the Planner adds to a place shows on the line as it stands (_Door to The Vault, locked_): no difficulty and no trap shows before a check is made on it. **Picking a lock** is a check on that way, rolled by whoever's line on the Story says they're picking it, with their own skill and tools (a rogue with thieves' tools rolls their Sleight of hand), against the door's own difficulty. On a success the line says the way _now stands open_. A character trained in the skill adds their proficiency to the pick (a rogue is trained in Sleight of hand: _+ 2 (proficiency)_). A failed pick on a trapped way springs the trap on the picker: the trap carries its own effect, which the Planner writes when it makes the way (the dice it deals and the condition it leaves). The table rolls the damage off their health and lays the condition on them, the line says what it did (_poison needle springs on Rook Valen: Health −3, Poisoned_), and the Narrator is told the same. A trap with no effect written is only told. The world's ways out are kept on their places from the moment the world is built, so the Planner names each one exactly from the first round, and listing a place again as it stands changes nothing. A spell needs a slot of its level: with none left, it doesn't go off, and the line says so (_Ilse has no first-level slot left: the spell fails._).

**Items do what they say.** The rules carry out what an item does, never the Planner by hand:

- **A weapon that hits** rolls its damage, adds the modifier the attack was rolled with, and takes it off the health of whoever it was aimed at: _⚄ Mira's Rapier deals 8 piercing damage to Bo: Health −8 — 1d8 (5) + 3 (Dexterity modifier) = 8_. Aimed at a foe nobody keeps a sheet for, the line just says the damage (_… to the goblin_). A great hit doubles the dice. A miss deals nothing.
- **Finesse and ranged weapons** (a rapier, a dagger, a bow) are used with Strength or Dexterity, whichever is better, for the attack and its damage alike.
- **A potion of healing** heals its dice, never past full health, and is gone: _⚄ Mira's Potion of healing heals 7: Health +7_. Given to someone else, it heals them.
- **An antitoxin** ends Poisoned (and Envenomed), and a poison the same round would have put on doesn't take hold.
- **A thrown flask** (alchemist's fire) harms whoever it's thrown at, and is used up whether it hits or not.
- **A healing spell** heals its dice plus the caster's spellcasting modifier; an attack spell that hits deals its dice; a spell that hits without a roll (Magic Missile) deals them when cast. What a spell costs is its own line.

Every use of something that runs out counts one off it, or one off the stack: _Mira uses Potion of healing; 1 left_. Two uses in one round each read what the first left, so a third drink from a stack of two finds none.

The rolls are real and repeatable. Nobody dies without a roll. A change the rules refuse (setting a number they work out themselves, say) isn't silently fixed: it waits as a suggestion in the session's review list.

## The character sheet

What a character's sheet holds is the session's **rules'**: the crawl brings no stats of its own. It **plays best with** a health and a level to count, and weapons, armour, gear, things used up, spells, what training lets someone do and what ails someone; rules without them play the crawl in words (see [Other rules](#other-rules)). Under Vis Itineris, the default, each character has:

- **Health**, drawn as a bar out of the character's own full health (_9/15_). The Planner and Rest read the same number. Rules that keep no health (Freeform, Wherewithal) have none: harm is told in words.
- **Armour class**: how hard they are to hit, worked out from their armour and Dexterity (chain mail and a shield: 18), shown right under Health. It is never stored, so it's always right, and you can't edit it. The Planner reads the same number on their sheet. Only at a table whose How strict counts numbers (**Rules** or **The rules decide**).
- **Level**, which rises as they earn it. Each level gained raises full health by what the rules give their class a level (under Vis Itineris, half the class's hit die plus one, plus the Constitution modifier: 8 for a fighter), and health with it. Rules that keep no levels raise nothing.
- **Class**: what kind of adventurer they are.
- **Weapons**, **Armour**, **Gear**, **Consumables**, **Spells** and **Features**: items and talents from the rules, each with what it does. A new character gets their class's starting kit; the story adds and takes away from there.
- **Conditions**: what ails them, and for how long.
- **Spell slots** (at a table playing spell slots, the default) or **Spell points** (at a table playing spell points; see [The rules' options](#the-rules-options)): what a caster casts with. A sheet shows only the one the table plays: switch Casting and the other row goes, and comes back, with what it held, if you switch again.
- Vis Itineris's own: the six abilities, trained skills and the rest of its sheet, whichever its options keep.

The crawl keeps a few things itself, whatever the rules, and shows them under the rules' stats (unless the sheet's **The game's own state** is set to **Hidden**):

- **Location**: where they are, in the sheet's **Where and when** row. Characters can be in different places, so this belongs to each of them. Everyone starts in the first place the Planner built, and it changes when the story moves them.
- **Standing order**: what they do on a round their player skips. Shown once the session has two seats.
- **Fate**: _Alive_, _Dying_ (rolling death saves, at a table playing them), _Taken out_ or _Dead_. While a character is dying, a **Death saves** row beside it keeps the count (_1 pass, 0 failures_); the row isn't there the rest of the time.
- The lists the crawl keeps their kit in: **Weapons**, **Armour**, **Gear**, **Consumables**, **Spells**, **Spell slots**, **Features** and **Conditions**.

An empty row on the sheet reads _none_, whatever kind of row it is.

The session itself keeps the **Time** (said as the day of the adventure and the hour, _Day 2, 16:30_, the world's **Where and when**), the **Difficulty**, whether the **Goal** is shared or secret, and the world's **Fuses**: things that will happen at a certain time unless someone stops them. Each place in the world can carry its **Ways out**: a door, a passage or a road, whether it's open, locked or hidden, any trap on it, and how long it takes to cross.

Companions keep no sheet yet (see [Companions](#companions)). See [Stats and states](./stats-and-states.md).

**Who can change a sheet by hand.** The session's owner can change anyone's sheet in the character sheet panel, any time. A player's own sheet is drawn read-only there until the owner turns on **Honour mode** in the session's settings; until then, ask the Planner in your Planner channel to change your standing order, and it does; your items change in play. A hand edit with Honour mode off is refused: _Sheets are the Planner's to change — ask in the Planner panel, or turn on Honour mode._ The Planner's own changes are never held back by the setting. Nobody but the owner can change another player's sheet.

## The rules' options

The session's **Rules options** (in its settings) offer Vis Itineris's choices. Two of them change how a crawl plays:

- **Casting: Spell points.** Instead of a few slots of each level, a caster has one pool of points: a spell costs its level in points (a cantrip nothing), and with too few left it can't be cast (_Not changed: Mira hasn't the spell points left to cast fireball._). A new wizard, cleric or bard starts with 4; a Rest fills the pool back up to what their class and level allow. A slot spent at a spell-points table is refused in words.
- **At zero health: Death saves.** A character brought to 0 health doesn't get the one roll at death's door. They lie **dying**, and roll a bare d20 every round: 10 or more is a pass, under 10 a failure, a natural 1 two failures, a natural 20 back on their feet with 1 health. Three passes and they are **stable**: out of the fight until the party rests. Three failures and they die (on Gentle, they're taken out instead). While they're dying they can do nothing, and healing them (a potion given to them, a healing spell) brings them back. The table line shows each save: _☠ Mira at death's door: death save d20 14 vs 10 — still dying (1 pass, 0 failures)_. Their sheet's Fate reads **Dying**, with the count beside it, and the journal says _Mira lies dying (1 pass, 0 failures)._ until they're stable, back up or dead.

With the default options (spell slots, and **At zero health: One roll at death's door**) the crawl plays as described everywhere else on this page: Fair and Brutal keep their one roll at death's door. Those are the two choices **At zero health** offers.

**The Story rules.** A session can play by the Story rules instead (words only, no numbers); the new-session form then plays **Words only** and doesn't ask How strict. Then the sheet keeps everything in words, with no number anywhere it draws:

- **Health**, **Level**, **Class** and **Spell slots** aren't kept at all: the Story rules declare no attributes, so the sheet says _This session's rules declare no attributes_ and harm is told in the story.
- The starting kit, a potion bought, a poison caught each land on the sheet as their name and a line saying what they are (_Mace — A heavy flanged club._, _Poisoned — Sick and shaky._).

The changes under each reply read in words too. The Planner is asked in plain words, with none of Vis Itineris's terms, and its notes on each character name them and where they stand (_Mira, at The Sunken Gate_), with no health or class. With no health kept, nobody reaches death's door, and a rest is told without anyone recovering.

### Other rules

The crawl reads the session's rules as they are, each time it needs them:

- **Checks** are asked by the rules' own names and decided their way: rolled where they roll, spent or weighed where they spend or weigh (Wherewithal's dares and contests).
- **What can change**: the Planner proposes changes to what the rules keep (their fields and their kinds of item), as well as health, class and where you are.
- **Classes and starting kits** are the rules' own. Rules without classes give a new character no kit.
- **A rest** takes as long as the rules' longest rest. Rules whose rests take no set time leave the clock where it is.
- **Death's door** is the rules' rule for falling. Rules without one roll nothing: a character brought down is taken out until the party rests.
- **Levels** raise full health only where the rules keep levels.
- **Ways out** keep a lock or a trap's difficulty only where the rules decide checks and have ways out of their own; elsewhere they're told in words.

## Companions

The characters you added to the session travel with the party **in the story only**, for now: the Narrator voices them, the opening names them and the world is built around them, but no sheet keeps their health or their things, and they never take a seat. A change the Planner asks for on one is a **Not changed** line.

*(Not built yet.)* Companions with sheets of their own, opinions, memory of how they're treated, and the choice to leave.

## Goal & leads, and the journal

Both are shared by the whole party, not one per character:

- **Goal & leads**: the goal, or _Kept secret by the Planner — ask in the Planner panel._ while it's being kept from you, and the open leads: short hooks the party could follow next, never a summary of what happened. Once the owner reveals the planning notes, the real goal shows here too. The planning notes always start with the goal.
- **Journal**: after each round, one line saying what's new, in character, then a smaller line for each place the round found (_Found The Stair._), each person it met (_Met Ila._), what changed (a condition wearing off, a roll at death's door), the time it took (_Day 1, 08:00 → Day 1, 09:30._) and each new lead; and one line for each rest. A round whose summary only repeats a recent line gets no summary line. The panel's **Line kinds** setting (a box for each kind) picks which of these show; it starts with every kind ticked but the time lines, which you can tick on. The Planner reads the newest forty lines as the story so far.

## Rest and time passing

Pick **Rest** from the composer's actions. The party rests for the rules' night (under Vis Itineris, its long rest: eight hours): everyone alive goes back to their own full health, anyone taken out gets back up, spent spell slots come back (or, at a spell-points table, every caster's pool fills up again), and the clock moves on by that long. No model call; the Story gets a short table line saying so, under its newest line (deleting that line takes the rest back with it), and the journal gets one too (_The party rests. Day 1, 16:30 → Day 2, 00:30._). The Planner hears about the rest on the next turn, from the journal.

When the rules have a Rest of their own (Vis Itineris does), that's the **Rest** you see: the rules restore what they restore, and the crawl's night follows. Anyone can take it for their own character, but only the session owner's Rest is the party's night. When the rules have none, the crawl's own **Rest** is there instead, and only the session's owner can rest the party. Rules whose rests take no set time leave the clock where it is: the line says only that the party rests.

## Fixing a turn

There are three ways to fix a turn, and they do different things. Only the latest turn can be fixed: the Narrator's newest reply on the Story, with the lines everyone sent for that round.

- **Swipe** the Narrator's reply for a new telling of the same turn. The Planner isn't asked again and the dice stand: same roll line, same results, same changes to your sheet and the world. Only the words change. **Extend** works the same way.
- **The opening scene can't be swiped or retried.** No turn wrote it (the Planner built the world and the Narrator opened it at the end of the interview), so there is nothing to tell again: _This message is not a reply — no reply wrote it, so there is nothing to generate again._ Edit it if you want different words.
- **A swipe that fails** (the model answers nothing) leaves the reply marked failed, and your sheet, the clock and where everyone stands go back to how they were before the swipe. A failed reply is never read back to the Planner or the Narrator.
- **A turn that fails** takes back what it changed (the clock, the sheet), and its row offers **Regenerate**: the whole turn, played again. A reply stopped before its first word reads *Stopped before a word* and is never read back either.
- **Regenerate the last turn** (the third press in the session's header, after Back and Forward; on a phone, the third item of the header's **⋯** menu) takes the whole turn after your line, every reply of it, as Back does, and plays it again from the start. It asks first (*Regenerate the last turn?*), and under **On revision: Branch** it branches first and plays the turn there. The Planner plans it again and the dice are rolled again, so you get a new roll line and new results. Everything the turn wrote goes with it (the sheet, the clock, its journal line, its rolls, and any place, person or cairn it added to the world), then the new turn's changes land. Your own lines stay. Only the session's owner can regenerate a turn (on your own, that's you). It is grey, with its reason, while a reply is being written or when there is no turn to regenerate. See [Sessions › Regenerate the last turn](./sessions.md#regenerate-the-last-turn). Regenerating one reply on its own isn't offered on the Story: a turn is always taken again whole.
- **Back** (at the top of the story) takes the whole latest round out of the story: everyone's lines, the Narrator's reply, its roll lines and journal line, and everything it changed (the sheet, the clock, anything it added to the world). **Forward** brings it back. Writing anything new replaces the rounds ahead for good. Only the session's owner presses them. See [Sessions › Back and Forward](./sessions.md#back-and-forward).
- **Delete** is off in the Dungeon Crawler: no message offers it, on the story or in your Planner channel. Hide a line, or use Back. A table that adds a deletion pipeline (see [Session actions](./session-actions.md#deleting-a-message-needs-a-pipeline)) gets the crawl's turn rule: the latest turn only, in **Honour mode** (_Lets players edit their own sheets by hand, and the host delete the latest turn where deleting is on. Off: neither._), only as the session's owner, and the whole turn at once; an older turn never (_Only the latest turn can be deleted — retake from there instead._).

With **On revision** set to **Branch**, regenerating or deleting the latest turn moves you to the new branch, where the turn is taken again (or the round waits for you); the original keeps the old turn. The branch starts from the sheets as they stood before that turn. Everything about the table comes along: every player, each person's Planner channel (each still sees only their own), the crawl's progress, and each person's layout of the session with its panels' settings (the tab names, the Planner tab on their own channel, the goal panel, the sheet). If you delete or hide your line before the round resolves, you're simply no longer counted as having declared.

## Death and endings

- **Death**: a character brought to 0 health is at death's door and gets one roll by the rules' own rule for falling (under Vis Itineris, 10 or more on Fair, 8 on Gentle, 12 on Brutal), unless the table plays **Death saves** (see [The rules' options](#the-rules-options)). Rules with no rule for falling roll nothing: the character is taken out of the fight until the party rests. Making it leaves them on 1 health, and their sheet's change line says so (_Maren Holt survives at death's door on 1 health_). Failing on **Gentle** takes them out of the fight until the party rests; on Fair or Brutal they die. A cairn for them stays in the world (the Planner remembers who fell, and where), and your Planner channel offers **Make a new character**: the new one arrives near where the old one fell, within a couple of rounds.
- **A new character** is a new seat, under the new character's own name. The fallen keeps theirs: its lines on the Story keep its name, and its cairn stands. If you were playing a persona, the persona card stays with the fallen too, and the session's settings list it under **The fallen** (its lines stay its own: there is nothing to reassign). From then on you write as the new character, so your lines on the Story, the round list and the Planner all name them, and your lines start a round as anyone's do.
- **Reaching the goal**: the Narrator closes the chapter, a secret goal shows in **Goal & leads**, every player is told in their Planner channel, and the session's owner is asked in theirs: **Carry on**, **A new goal** (the Planner sets one that grows out of the story) or **End the adventure**. **End** has the Narrator close the adventure on the Story straight away. After the end, the session stays readable and a turn only gets a short epilogue. Someone who joins after the end is told so in their Planner channel (_This adventure has ended: the Story and the Journal stay here to read._) and isn't asked to make a character.

## Revealing the planning notes

The Planner keeps secret notes: what is really going on, what is hidden where, what the world will do next. When the session's owner asks the Planner to reveal them, they appear for everyone under **Planning notes**, and stay there. Revealing can't be undone.

To reveal them, the owner presses **Reveal the planning notes** among the composer's actions (only the owner sees it). It asks first (_Reveal the planning notes?_), because it can't be taken back: **Reveal** goes ahead, **Cancel** changes nothing. The Planner posts a short note on the Story, under its own name, saying the notes are out. That note doesn't count as a turn, so any declarations already made this round still stand. Asking a second time gets a polite reply in the owner's Planner channel, and nothing changes.

## What it doesn't do yet

- Everything marked *(not built yet)* above, and companions with opinions of their own.
- A map of the world.
- Changing a place or a person once they're in the world: the Planner adds new places, ways and people, but a place's description, or a person's mood, stays as first written.
- The goal and the Planner's notes, and the open leads, are kept beside the world rather than in it for now.
- Sessions started before the rules arrived keep their characters and goal, but their old places and people aren't carried over: the Planner starts the map again from where you are.
- Starting kits come from the rules' own classes: rules with none (Wherewithal, Freeform) start a character with what the interview gave, and nothing more.
- Splitting the party into separate scenes. For now everyone shares one story, and anything your character couldn't know goes to your Planner channel.
- Starting from one of your lorebooks.
